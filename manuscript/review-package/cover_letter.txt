Dear Editor,

Please consider "Sensitivity-aware inspection prioritisation for archaeological landscapes: a reproducible Taxila observatory" for the Journal of Cultural Heritage.

The study addresses how conservation teams can translate heterogeneous geospatial observations into a defensible sequence of field inspections. Using the Taxila World Heritage property, it combines multi-epoch satellite observations, terrain descriptors and acquisition-matched weather with explicit tests of spatial support, indicator dependence and rank uncertainty. A separate buffered land-cover benchmark tests geographic proxy agreement without conflating it with monument condition.

The methodological contribution is an explicit evaluation of the inspection rule. Mathematical definitions connect hierarchical indicator integration to exact decision-weight transitions, shared spatial-block resampling, positional perturbations and nested spatial-decision simulation. The main article presents the central experiments, including a seven-family spatial comparison and probability diagnostics. The revised framework diagram and three-dimensional rank-response view make the dependencies between observations, assumptions and management recommendations visible.

The study is positioned alongside recent Taxila risk and land-cover research and the journal's 2026 work on the landscape context of World Heritage climate vulnerability. Its output is a component-specific inspection portfolio with explicit evidence limits. Supplementary tables, machine-readable results and executable code document the computations supporting the main article.
