To the Editors, Journal of Cultural Heritage

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration "Sensitivity-aware inspection prioritisation for
archaeological landscapes: a reproducible Taxila observatory" as an original
research article.

The problem we address is a scheduling problem that site authorities face
everywhere and that the literature addresses only indirectly. Environmental
screening can order the components of a serial property by inferred pressure, but
published orderings almost never report how much of the order is a property of
the landscape and how much is a property of the analysis. A site manager handed a
ranking cannot tell an exposed component from an artefact of a weighting choice,
and gets no guidance on whether to resolve a preference or to acquire better data.

Our contribution is to answer those questions for the Taxila World Heritage
property, and to do so with a method that transfers. We show that the joint
spatial-decision simulation underlying such a framework admits an exact affine
reconstruction: the latent landscape and terrain terms are identified by the
recorded draws themselves, to machine precision. That converts 5,000 Monte Carlo
draws into a balanced 280,998-scenario factorial design at no additional
computational cost, and yields three results a ranking cannot express. No
component reaches a first-rank confidence factor above 0.42. Decision assumptions
account for 45.8% of rank variance against 31.8% for spatial support, and the
split inverts between components, so the analysis prescribes per component
whether a weighting decision or a data acquisition would settle its rank. And an
inspection budget curve shows that visiting the three core-tier components covers
a scenario's own three highest priorities in only 17.9% of cases, against 51.8%
for four - which prices the recommendation in site visits rather than asserting a
tier.

We also report a result that does not favour our framework, and we consider it
the most important one in the paper. We confronted the ordering with the
independent published evidence for Taxila. None of the five components for which
damage or encroachment has been documented by Khan et al. (2022, this journal) or
Butt et al. (2025) falls within our core tier, and the component with the
clearest documented loss of designated area cannot be scored at all because its
inventory coordinate is unresolved. Rather than omit this, we have made it the
paper's framing: environmental screening of this kind measures moisture- and
terrain-driven landscape pressure, which is a different construct from documented
fabric damage or built-up encroachment, and it must be combined with damage
records rather than substituted for them. We believe a conservation readership is
better served by a screening tool with a stated scope than by a risk ranking with
an implied one.

The work is fully reproducible. Every number, figure and table derives from an
archived evidence package with a SHA-256 manifest covering 158 files, and the
decision-analysis scripts are included. All results were independently
re-executed on two platforms.

The manuscript is 4,993 words excluding tables and references, with ten display
items (eight figures, two tables) and a Research aim section of 88 words. It has
not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. Author
identity is confined to the separate title page, so the manuscript file is
suitable for anonymised review.
