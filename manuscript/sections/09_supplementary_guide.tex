\section{Scope and interpretation boundary}

This Supplementary Information is the evidence-routing guide for the extended Taxila manuscript. It identifies the complete records that support the condensed methods, results and displays in the main paper. The analytical outputs describe relative landscape pressure, terrain and hydrological susceptibility, regional climate forcing and relative field-inspection priority. They do not demonstrate monument damage, condition, structural safety, annual loss probability or a legally defined risk zone. The 250, 500 and 1,000~m circles are analytical supports rather than official property or buffer boundaries. WorldCover-derived classes are proxy land-cover labels and are not independent heritage-condition observations.

The final validated experiment package is authoritative. Legacy summaries and remembered values must not be used when they differ from the claim-evidence matrix, discrepancy record or final tables. Saraikala remains in the documentary inventory but is excluded from coordinate-dependent summaries because its geometry is unresolved.

\section{S2: Landsat record, quality control and common support}

This section must preserve the complete 55-scene inventory, sensor allocation, three-year post-monsoon windows, quality-flag exclusions, Collection~2 scaling, valid-observation counts and common-support construction. Landsat surface-reflectance provenance and cross-sensor interpretation are anchored by established processing and product studies \citep{masek2006ledaps,vermote2016landsat,roy2016landsat}. The workflow used Collection~2 quality flags; Fmask literature documents the broader importance of cloud and shadow control but does not describe an extra algorithm executed in this study \citep{zhu2012fmask,zhu2015fmask}. Denser breakpoint and trajectory methods provide methodological context for what a five-epoch design cannot resolve \citep{verbesselt2010bfast,kennedy2010landtrendr}.

\section{S3: Spectral transformations and convergence}

This section must give the executed equations, band mapping, pressure direction, normalisation, percentile thresholds and common-support denominator. It must preserve exact endpoint thresholds and the counts for zero, one, two and three convergent criteria. The full component-by-epoch summaries belong here. Captions must call the outputs landscape pressure or spectral convergence, never condition, damage or risk.

\section{S4: Climate provenance, trends and product sensitivity}

Figures~S1--S4 and Tables~S3--S4 provide the complete monthly, seasonal, annual, extreme, baseline-sensitivity, product-comparison and acquisition-window records. ERA5 and ERA5-Land publications establish the reanalysis context but do not convert a gridded product into local station truth \citep{hersbach2020era5,munozsabater2021era5land}. Trend interpretation must retain the consequences of serial dependence and multiple comparisons \citep{hamed1998mk,yue2002autocorrelation,wilks2016stippling}. The moving-block design is a dependence-preserving resampling choice, not an assertion that five years is the uniquely correct block length \citep{politis1994stationary}. All five-epoch weather-spectral correlations remain exploratory, without causal language or formal significance claims.

\section{S8: Geographic proxy-label experiment}

Table~S7 must preserve development, buffer and test sample counts, group definitions, tuning records, all model metrics, class-wise confusion, calibration and block-bootstrap intervals. Spatial validation must be aligned with the geographic transfer claim \citep{hijmans2012cv,meyer2018target,meyer2019selection,meyer2021aoa,roberts2017cv}. \section{S9: Heritage interpretation and field use}

The supplementary interpretation record must distinguish screening from complete risk assessment, and it must keep landscape change separate from material condition. Heritage Earth-observation studies support repeated landscape surveillance and change detection \citep{agapiou2017petabyte,agapiou2021dynamic,tapete2017sar}. Climate-vulnerability and adaptation research further shows that usable conservation decisions require vulnerability, capacity, local knowledge and field evidence beyond remote forcing layers \citep{sesana2020vulnerability,crowley2022risk,daly2022adaptation}. These sources support the paper's conservative field-inspection endpoint; they do not supply missing condition labels for Taxila.

\section{S10: Reproducibility and experiment disposition}

The final supplementary archive must include or cross-reference the source manifest, scene and weather inventories, data dictionary, configurations, random seed, dependency record, execution logs, checksums, claim-evidence audit, equation-code map, display-source map, discrepancy record, result-disposition matrix and limitations register. Fourteen experiments were completed, the weather-spectral association experiment remained exploratory, and the ancillary-hazard experiment was not executed. Earthquake, flood-event, landslide, quarry and population layers must not appear as completed analyses.

\section{Caption and reporting checklist}

Every final display must define abbreviations, units, denominator, uncertainty and analysis scale. Maps must state the coordinate reference system and identify whether they represent pressure, susceptibility or priority. Captions must explain NoData and source provenance. Table notes must distinguish fixed scores, ranks, percentile intervals and probabilities. No caption may use damage, condition accuracy or risk unless the displayed analysis independently supports that term. Final insertion, numbering and cross-referencing of the verified files are Phase~8 assembly tasks.
