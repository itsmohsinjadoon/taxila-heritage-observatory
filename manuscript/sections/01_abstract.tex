\begin{abstract}
Conservation teams need to prioritise inspections across archaeological landscapes using environmental observations collected at different spatial and temporal scales. We present the Climate-contextual Heritage Inspection Prioritisation (CHIP) framework and evaluate how its recommendations change under alternative indicator, spatial-support and uncertainty assumptions at the Taxila World Heritage property, Pakistan. The framework combines 55 Landsat scenes across five epochs, approximately 30 m terrain data, daily weather for 1991--2025 and 17 geolocated heritage components. A hierarchical landscape--terrain score is evaluated through domain and factor ablation, three neighbourhood radii, 27 structural scenarios, spatial-block resampling, coordinate perturbation and joint spatial--decision simulation. Of 386,280 common-support cells, 16.93\% met at least two adverse spectral criteria. Removing MNDWI changed rank concordance to $\rho=-0.280$, whereas removing NDVI or NDBI retained $\rho>0.989$, showing that information content was unevenly distributed across indicators. Giri led the fixed 500 m ranking and entered the top three in 73.24\% of joint score vectors; its 95\% rank interval nevertheless spanned 1--9. Spatial reconstruction and decision assumptions produced distinct inspection rationales, particularly for Bhallar. Opposing precipitation trends in Open-Meteo and NASA POWER limited climatic attribution. In a separate seven-family spatial land-cover comparison, development-selected random forest achieved macro-F1 0.821 (95\% interval 0.691--0.835); feature controls and calibration experiments exposed limitations that a single accuracy score would conceal. CHIP translates heterogeneous observations into component-specific inspection priorities while quantifying the conditions under which those priorities change. Its contribution is an experimentally evaluated decision-support procedure for landscape screening, with monument-condition validation remaining a subsequent field task.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cultural heritage; Taxila; remote sensing; spatial uncertainty; inspection prioritisation; reproducibility.
