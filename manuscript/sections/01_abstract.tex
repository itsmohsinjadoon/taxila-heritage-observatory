\begin{abstract}
Heritage authorities must allocate scarce inspection capacity across serial
properties whose components differ in exposure, accessibility and evidence
quality. Environmental screening from satellite, terrain and reanalysis data can
order components by inferred pressure, but it rarely reports whether that order
survives the analyst's own choices, or whether it agrees with independent
evidence of condition. We present the Climate-contextual Heritage Inspection
Prioritisation (CHIP) framework and subject its recommendation for the Taxila
World Heritage property, Pakistan, to systematic decision analysis. Inputs are 55
Landsat scenes across five epochs, approximately 30~m terrain, daily weather for
1991--2025 and 17 of 18 inventory components. Of 386,280 common-support cells,
16.93\% met at least two adverse endpoint criteria against 10.40\% expected under
independence (ratio 1.63), so adverse signals co-occur rather than disperse.
Indicator influence was strongly asymmetric: removing NDVI or NDBI retained
$\rho>0.989$, whereas removing MNDWI gave $\rho=-0.280$ (95\% CI $-0.676$ to
0.242). Across eight aggregation rules, an oriented principal-component rule
reversed the ordering ($\rho=-0.771$, $-0.925$ to $-0.403$). Recovering the exact
affine score geometry from the joint simulation converted 5,000 draws into a
balanced 280,998-scenario design: no component exceeded a first-rank confidence
factor of 0.42; decision assumptions accounted for 45.8\% of rank variance
against 31.8\% for spatial support; and the per-component split inverted, from
81.5\% decision-driven for Giri Mosque and tombs to 60.6\% support-driven for
Kalawan. A three-component programme covered a scenario's three highest
priorities in only 17.9\% of cases, while a fourth component raised this to
51.8\%. None of the five Taxila components with independent published
documentation of damage or encroachment fell within the CHIP core tier, and the
one component with documented core-zone loss could not be scored because its
coordinate is unresolved. CHIP therefore supplies a defensible,
uncertainty-annotated screening layer for moisture- and terrain-driven landscape
pressure. That construct differs from documented fabric damage and from built-up
encroachment, and should be combined with them rather than substituted for them.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cultural heritage; Taxila; inspection prioritisation;
decision analysis under uncertainty; rank acceptability; remote sensing; reproducibility.
