\section{Results}
\subsection{Multitemporal landscape pressure and spectral convergence}
Landscape-pressure patterns varied across the five epochs (Fig.~\ref{fig:epochs}). 386,280 of 403,480 cells (95.74\%) were valid at both endpoints, leaving 17,200 cells outside change denominators. On this intersection, 65,391 cells (16.93\%) met at least two adverse criteria and 5,493 (1.42\%) met all three. The empirical thresholds were $\Delta$NDVI $\leq-0.00543$, $\Delta$MNDWI $\leq-0.03743$ and $\Delta$NDBI $\geq0.00835$. Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} locates their convergence relative to the wider pressure pattern.

Because the three criteria are defined at fixed quantiles of this same cell set, each marginal rate is exactly 0.200 by construction and the co-occurrence rates expected under independence follow analytically. Observed co-occurrence exceeded that reference: 16.93\% against 10.40\% for two or more criteria (ratio 1.63) and 1.42\% against 0.80\% for all three (1.78), while the rate for at least one criterion fell below it (41.65\% against 48.80\%). The adverse signals therefore concentrate in shared cells rather than dispersing independently, which is what makes a spatially targeted inspection programme worth constructing.

At 500 m, NDVI- and NDBI-pressure ranks were strongly aligned ($\rho=0.972$), so their grouping governs how surface-cover information enters the hierarchy. Giri's surface-cover term was low (0.0176) while its MNDWI-pressure percentile was 0.9941 and terrain score 0.6765: its leading priority arose from the moisture-related and terrain terms, not from agreement among all indicators.

\subsection{Aggregation rules and ablations identify the drivers of priority}
Giri complex led the fixed 500 m score (0.5912), followed by Giri Mosque and tombs (0.5725) and Jaulian (0.5676). All comparisons below are Spearman coefficients against this hierarchical reference on $n=17$ components, with 95\% intervals; Supplementary Table~S12 reports the complete set.

The ordering proved conditional on the aggregation family, not merely on the weights within it. Two alternatives preserved it (CRITIC $\rho=0.972$, 0.905--0.992; entropy $\rho=0.748$, 0.359--0.916) and the ungrouped equal-factor mean largely did so ($\rho=0.844$, 0.555--0.951; three of the reference top five; Bhallar first). Two departed from it entirely: a robust-$z$ logistic aggregation of the same six factors was unrelated to the hierarchical ranking ($\rho=0.038$, $-0.451$ to 0.509), and an oriented first principal component reversed it ($\rho=-0.771$, $-0.925$ to $-0.403$), sharing no top-five component and placing Bhir Mound---last of 17 in the reference---first. The ordering is therefore a property of additively aggregating oriented percentiles, not recoverable by variance- or covariance-driven aggregation of the same evidence. Landscape-only scoring gave $\rho=0.325$ ($-0.198$ to 0.704) and terrain-only $\rho=0.830$ (0.523--0.946); on 17 components neither is separable from the equal-factor baseline.

Factor ablations were markedly asymmetric (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a). Removing NDVI or NDBI retained $\rho=0.991$ (0.969--0.998) and 0.990 (0.963--0.997) and left Giri first. Removing MNDWI destroyed concordance with the reference: $\rho=-0.280$ ($-0.676$ to 0.242), no top-five overlap and a maximum displacement of 13 positions. The interval spans zero, so the evidence supports loss of concordance rather than a negative association. Removing slope gave $\rho=0.611$ (0.139--0.857) and removing drainage proximity $\rho=0.454$ ($-0.061$ to 0.778), both placing Sirsukh first; removing the wetness term retained $\rho=0.892$ (0.671--0.967). The hierarchy thus reduced duplication between the vegetation and built/bare terms, while the moisture-related indicator and the terrain pathways remained consequential to the decision.

\subsection{Spatial support, structural choices and inspection tiers}
Rank agreement was stronger between 500 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.918$) than between 250 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.711$; Fig.~\ref{fig:scales}). Jaulian ranked 2, 3 and 1 across the three radii, Giri complex 4, 1 and 2, and Giri Mosque and tombs moved from first at 250 m to fourth at 1,000 m.

Across the 27 structural scenarios, Giri complex, Giri Mosque and tombs, and Jaulian formed the core tier (top three in 17, 20 and 18 scenarios), and Khader Mohra and Mohra Moradu the conditional tier (top five in 15 and 17). Exact intervals show the two tiers are resolved unequally. Core-tier top-five frequencies are well separated from the threshold (Jaulian 24/27, 0.889, 95\% CI 0.708--0.976; Giri complex and Giri Mosque 23/27, 0.852, 0.663--0.958), whereas Khader Mohra's membership rests on 15/27 (0.556, 0.353--0.745), straddling the 0.5 threshold that defines the tier and overlapping Dharmarajika's Tier~3 value (11/27, 0.407, 0.224--0.612). The core tier is supported at this evidence level; the conditional tier is a screening device that 27 scenarios do not settle (Supplementary Table~S6).

Exact crossings sharpen this interpretation (Eq.~\ref{eq:crossing}): at 500 m Giri leads for $0.1047<w<0.7692$, whereas at 250 m leadership passes to Giri Mosque and tombs at 0.2941 and at 1,000 m Jaulian leads until 0.7143, so a broad weight interval at one radius does not imply leadership across radii. Exact uniform-weight top-three frequencies matched the 50,000-draw experiment within 0.0024; Supplementary Table~S13 lists all intervals.

\subsection{Spatial, positional and joint uncertainty}
The three perturbation designs disagree informatively (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b--d). Shared-block resampling left Giri's top-three frequency at 0.960--0.994 while Giri Mosque and tombs fell from 0.773 to 0.390; the undisplaced re-extraction control already ranked Bhallar first, before any displacement; and joint simulation gave Giri median rank 2 (interval 1--9, top-three frequency 0.7324) and Bhallar median rank 3 (1--13, 0.5260). Bhallar's prominence under reconstruction contrasts with its persistence across the fixed scenarios (2/27). Feature reconstruction and positional uncertainty therefore make separable contributions (Supplementary Section~S7, Table~S6).

\subsection{Decision analysis of the reconstructed design}
Affine reconstruction (Eq.~\eqref{eq:affine}) identified the latent landscape and terrain terms exactly, with a maximum residual of $6.6\times10^{-15}$, and returned a balanced factorial of 280,998 scenarios over 466 of the 500 recorded spatial states. The reconstruction reproduces the original simulation: top-three acceptability for Giri complex was 0.7333 against 0.7324 from the 5,000 draws, and 0.5343, 0.4604 and 0.4118 against 0.5260, 0.4626 and 0.4096 for Bhallar, Giri Mosque and tombs, and Jaulian.

Integrated priorities are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:priority}. Three results follow that the frequency summaries do not provide (Eq.~\eqref{eq:central}). First, first-rank and top-three acceptability disagree about the leader: Bhallar has the highest first-rank acceptability (0.310), Giri complex the highest top-three (0.733). Second, no component is a confident first priority: the largest confidence factor is 0.42 (Bhallar), then 0.37 (Giri complex) and 0.27 (Sirkap), so at its own central weight the best-supported component still fails to lead in over half of spatial states. Third, central weights separate the property into two inspection rationales (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}c): Bhallar, Giri complex and Giri Mosque and tombs lead only at low landscape weight ($w^{c}=0.375$, 0.382, 0.296), when terrain and moisture evidence dominates, whereas Sirkap, Kunala, Dharmarajika and Sirsukh lead only at high landscape weight (0.871, 0.859, 0.824, 0.896). Giri Mosque and tombs is the clearest conditional case: 99.8\% of its first-rank scenarios occur under the reduced slope--drainage formulation.

The variance decomposition (Eq.~\eqref{eq:vardecomp}) states which assumption to resolve first, and the answer is component-specific (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}a). Across the design, decision assumptions account for 45.8\% of rank variance (landscape weight 35.2\%, terrain formulation 8.5\%) against 31.8\% for spatial support, with 22.4\% interaction. Per component the split inverts. For Giri Mosque and tombs (81.5\% decision), Sirkap (75.6\%) and Giri complex (67.2\%), no improvement in spatial support would settle the rank; a declared weighting would. For Kalawan (60.6\% support), Khanpur Cave (57.1\%) and Pippala (49.6\%) the reverse holds. Bhallar is distinctive: the terrain formulation alone accounts for 29.3\% of its rank variance, the highest of any component, locating its earlier ambiguity in how terrain susceptibility is formulated.

The inspection-budget curve (Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage}) converts these distributions into a programme size (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}b). Visiting the three core-tier components leaves an expected 1.27 of a scenario's three highest priorities unvisited and covers all three in only 17.9\% of scenarios. Adding a fourth component, Jaulian, raises full coverage to 51.8\%---a gain of 33.9 percentage points, larger than every subsequent addition combined up to $k=8$; coverage then improves slowly (67.6\% at seven, 81.9\% at nine). The defensible core programme at Taxila is therefore four components rather than three.

\subsection{Agreement with independent component-level evidence}
Five Taxila components carry published component-level evidence of damage or encroachment (Table~\ref{tab:external}). Khan et al. document damage at Sirkap, Dharmarajika and Mohra Moradu \citep{khan2022taxila}; Butt et al. report built-up clustering adjacent to Bhir Mound and a reduction of the Saraikala core zone from 0.0168 to 0.0032~km$^2$ over 2004--2024 \citep{butt2025taxila}. CHIP ranks these at 4, 5, 12 and 17 of 17 respectively, and cannot score Saraikala at all. None falls within the core tier, and none exceeds a top-three acceptability of 0.17.

The disagreement is systematic and its direction matches the ablation evidence: CHIP prioritises components with high moisture-related and terrain-hydrological percentiles, whereas the externally documented components are those with fabric damage or adjacent built-up expansion. Giri complex shows the separation directly, with a surface-cover pressure term of 0.0176 against an MNDWI-pressure percentile of 0.9941. The two are measuring different constructs on the same property, so the CHIP ordering cannot be used as a general condition ranking. Saraikala is the sharpest instance: the component excluded for want of a coordinate is the one with documented core-zone loss.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition-matched conditions}
In Open-Meteo, annual precipitation decreased by 10.30 mm year$^{-1}$ (95\% residual-block interval $-17.91$ to $-2.67$; annual-family $q=0.027$), annual mean temperature rose by 0.0418~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$ (0.0099--0.0731; $q=0.026$) and heavy-precipitation days declined by 0.143 days year$^{-1}$ ($q=0.018$). NASA POWER gave opposing point slopes over the same period ($+29.21$ mm year$^{-1}$ and $-0.0372$~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$), and annual precipitation agreement was weak (Spearman $\rho=0.083$; RMSE 646.74 mm year$^{-1}$; Supplementary Fig.~S5). The two products do not support a common regional narrative, so no climatic attribution of the landscape pattern is offered.

Acquisition-window matching differentiated the epochs further: E2024 antecedent precipitation averaged 42.47, 221.19 and 852.92 mm over 30, 90 and 365 days, but the maxima fell in E2004, E2019 and E2014 respectively. Weather preceding the retained scenes therefore cannot be summarised by a single wet or dry label per epoch. Definitions and annual trends are in Supplementary Table~S2.

\subsection{Land-cover proxy comparison as a negative control}
Buffered development validation selected \ExtensionModel{} with mean macro-F1 \ExtensionCV{}; on the outer stripe it achieved macro-F1 \ExtensionFOne{} (95\% test-block interval \ExtensionLow{}--\ExtensionHigh{}) and agreement \ExtensionAgreement{}. Four of seven families exceeded that macro-F1 by 0.005--0.028, selection having been pre-declared and not revised on the outer stripe; bands-only logistic regression matched the selected model at 0.824, location-only, shuffled-label and class-prior controls reached 0.140, 0.070 and 0.049, and development-only temperature scaling raised calibration error from 0.014 to 0.136. Useful land-cover agreement is therefore attainable, is not improved by model complexity here, and---consistent with Table~\ref{tab:external}---does not constitute monument-condition accuracy (Supplementary Section~S5, Tables~S5--S10).
