\section{Results}
\subsection{Multitemporal landscape pressure and spectral convergence}
Landscape-pressure patterns varied across the five epochs (Fig.~\ref{fig:epochs}). 386,280 of 403,480 cells (95.74\%) were valid at both endpoints, leaving 17,200 cells outside change denominators. On this intersection, 65,391 cells (16.93\%) met at least two adverse criteria and 5,493 (1.42\%) met all three. The empirical thresholds were $\Delta$NDVI $\leq-0.00543$, $\Delta$MNDWI $\leq-0.03743$ and $\Delta$NDBI $\geq0.00835$. Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} locates their convergence relative to the wider pressure pattern.

Each adverse criterion marks 20\% of the common cell set by construction. Independence therefore predicts 10.40\% meeting at least two, 0.80\% all three and 48.80\% at least one; observed rates were 16.93\%, 1.42\% and 41.65\%. Adverse signals concentrate in shared cells rather than dispersing independently.

At 500 m, NDVI- and NDBI-pressure ranks were strongly aligned ($\rho=0.972$), so their grouping governs how surface-cover information enters the hierarchy. Giri's surface-cover term was low (0.0176) while its MNDWI-pressure percentile was 0.9941 and terrain score 0.6765: its leading priority arose from the moisture-related and terrain terms, not from agreement among all indicators.

\subsection{Aggregation rules and ablations identify the drivers of priority}
Giri complex led the fixed 500 m score (0.5912), followed by Giri Mosque and tombs (0.5725) and Jaulian (0.5676). All comparisons below are Spearman coefficients against this hierarchical reference on $n=17$ components, with 95\% intervals; Supplementary Table~S13 reports the complete set.

Aggregation changed the order more than weight changes within the hierarchy. CRITIC preserved it ($\rho=0.972$, 95\% interval 0.905--0.992), as did entropy ($\rho=0.748$, 0.359--0.916) and the ungrouped equal-factor mean ($\rho=0.844$, 0.555--0.951; Bhallar first). A robust-$z$ logistic rule was unrelated to the reference ($\rho=0.038$, $-0.451$ to 0.509), while the oriented first principal component reversed it ($\rho=-0.771$, $-0.925$ to $-0.403$), sharing no top-five component and placing reference-last Bhir Mound first. Landscape-only scoring gave $\rho=0.325$ ($-0.198$ to 0.704) and terrain-only $\rho=0.830$ (0.523--0.946). Thus, the ordering depends on the aggregation family (Supplementary Table~S13).

Factor ablations were markedly asymmetric (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a). Removing NDVI or NDBI retained $\rho=0.991$ (0.969--0.998) and 0.990 (0.963--0.997) and left Giri first. Removing MNDWI destroyed concordance with the reference: $\rho=-0.280$ ($-0.676$ to 0.242), no top-five overlap and a maximum displacement of 13 positions. The interval spans zero, so the evidence supports loss of concordance rather than a negative association. Removing slope gave $\rho=0.611$ (0.139--0.857) and removing drainage proximity $\rho=0.454$ ($-0.061$ to 0.778), both placing Sirsukh first; removing the wetness term retained $\rho=0.892$ (0.671--0.967). The hierarchy thus reduced duplication between the vegetation and built/bare terms, while the moisture-related indicator and the terrain pathways remained consequential to the decision.

\subsection{Spatial support, structural choices and inspection tiers}
Rank agreement was stronger between 500 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.918$) than between 250 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.711$; Fig.~\ref{fig:scales}). Jaulian ranked 2, 3 and 1 across the three radii, Giri complex 4, 1 and 2, and Giri Mosque and tombs moved from first at 250 m to fourth at 1,000 m.

Across 27 structural scenarios, Giri complex, Giri Mosque and tombs, and Jaulian formed the core tier (top three in 17, 20 and 18 scenarios); Khader Mohra and Mohra Moradu formed the conditional tier (top five in 15 and 17). Core-tier top-five frequencies were 24/27 for Jaulian and 23/27 for each Giri component, with 95\% intervals above the 0.5 tier threshold. Khader Mohra's 15/27 (0.556, 0.353--0.745) straddled that threshold and overlapped Dharmarajika's 11/27 (0.407, 0.224--0.612). The 27 scenarios therefore do not settle conditional-tier membership (Supplementary Table~S5).

Exact crossings sharpen this interpretation (Eq.~\ref{eq:crossing}): at 500 m Giri leads for $0.1047<w<0.7692$, whereas at 250 m leadership passes to Giri Mosque and tombs at 0.2941 and at 1,000 m Jaulian leads until 0.7143, so a broad weight interval at one radius does not imply leadership across radii. Exact uniform-weight top-three frequencies matched the 50,000-draw experiment within 0.0024; Supplementary Table~S14 lists all intervals.

\subsection{Spatial, positional and joint uncertainty}
The perturbation designs differ (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b--d). Shared-block resampling left Giri's top-three frequency at 0.960--0.994 while Giri Mosque and tombs fell from 0.773 to 0.390; the undisplaced re-extraction control already ranked Bhallar first, before any displacement; and joint simulation gave Giri median rank 2 (interval 1--9, top-three frequency 0.7324) and Bhallar median rank 3 (1--13, 0.5260). Bhallar's prominence under reconstruction contrasts with its persistence across the fixed scenarios (2/27). Reconstruction and positional uncertainty make separable contributions (Supplementary Section~S8, Table~S4).

\subsection{Decision analysis of the reconstructed design}
Affine reconstruction (Eq.~\eqref{eq:affine}) identified the latent landscape and terrain terms exactly, with a maximum residual of $6.6\times10^{-15}$, and returned a balanced factorial of 280,998 scenarios over 466 of the 500 recorded spatial states. Top-three acceptability closely matched the 5,000-draw simulation: Giri complex 0.7333 versus 0.7324, and Bhallar 0.5343 versus 0.5260.

Figure~\ref{fig:priority} maps integrated priorities. Bhallar had the highest first-rank acceptability (0.310), but Giri complex had the highest top-three acceptability (0.733). No component was a confident first priority: the largest confidence factor was 0.42 (Bhallar), followed by 0.37 (Giri complex) and 0.27 (Sirkap). Central weights (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}c) separated terrain-led Bhallar, Giri complex and Giri Mosque and tombs ($w^{c}=0.375$, 0.382, 0.296) from landscape-led Sirkap, Kunala, Dharmarajika and Sirsukh (0.871, 0.859, 0.824, 0.896). Giri Mosque and tombs led almost exclusively under the reduced slope--drainage formulation (99.8\% of its first-rank scenarios).

Decision assumptions explained 45.8\% of rank variance (landscape weight 35.2\%, terrain formulation 8.5\%) versus 31.8\% for spatial support and 22.4\% interaction (Eq.~\eqref{eq:vardecomp}; Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}a). Decision shares dominated for Giri Mosque and tombs (81.5\%), Sirkap (75.6\%) and Giri complex (67.2\%); support dominated for Kalawan (60.6\%), Khanpur Cave (57.1\%) and Pippala (49.6\%). Bhallar had the largest terrain-formulation share (29.3\%). These splits indicate whether the next step is a declared weighting choice or better spatial support.

The inspection-budget curve (Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage}) converts these distributions into a programme size (Fig.~\ref{fig:decision}b). Visiting the first three components in acceptability order (Giri complex, Bhallar and Giri Mosque and tombs) leaves an expected 1.27 of a scenario's three highest priorities unvisited and covers all three in only 17.9\% of scenarios. Adding a fourth component, Jaulian, raises full coverage to 51.8\%---a gain of 33.9 percentage points, larger than every subsequent addition combined up to $k=8$; coverage then improves slowly (67.6\% at seven, 81.9\% at nine). Under the stated coverage criterion, the programme requires four visits.

\subsection{Agreement with independent component-level evidence}
Five Taxila components carry published component-level evidence of damage or encroachment (Table~\ref{tab:external}). Khan et al. document damage at Sirkap, Dharmarajika and Mohra Moradu \citep{khan2022taxila}; Butt et al. report built-up clustering adjacent to Bhir Mound and a reduction of the Saraikala core zone from 0.0168 to 0.0032~km$^2$ over 2004--2024 \citep{butt2025taxila}. CHIP ranks these at 4, 5, 12 and 17 of 17 respectively, and cannot score Saraikala at all. None falls within the core tier, and none exceeds a top-three acceptability of 0.17.

The disagreement matches the ablation evidence: CHIP responds chiefly to moisture-related and terrain-hydrological percentiles, while published damage and encroachment represent different constructs. Giri complex had a surface-cover pressure term of only 0.0176 against an MNDWI-pressure percentile of 0.9941. The CHIP order therefore cannot be used as a general condition ranking. Saraikala, excluded for lack of a coordinate, has documented core-zone loss.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition-matched conditions}
Open-Meteo precipitation declined by 10.30 mm year$^{-1}$ (95\% residual-block interval $-17.91$ to $-2.67$; $q=0.027$) and mean temperature rose by 0.0418~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$ (0.0099--0.0731; $q=0.026$). NASA POWER gave opposing point slopes ($+29.21$ mm year$^{-1}$ and $-0.0372$~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$), with weak precipitation agreement (Spearman $\rho=0.083$; Supplementary Fig.~S2). No regional climatic attribution is therefore offered.

Acquisition-window precipitation also varied: E2024 averaged 42.47, 221.19 and 852.92 mm over 30, 90 and 365 days, while the respective maxima occurred in E2004, E2019 and E2014. No single wet/dry label fits an epoch (Supplementary Table~S2).
