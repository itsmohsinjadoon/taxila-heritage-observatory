\section{Results}
\subsection{Multitemporal landscape pressure and spectral convergence}
Landscape-pressure patterns varied across the five epochs (Fig.~\ref{fig:epochs}). 386,280 of 403,480 cells (95.74\%) were valid at both endpoints, leaving 17,200 cells outside change denominators. On this intersection, 65,391 cells (16.93\%) met at least two adverse criteria and 5,493 (1.42\%) met all three. The empirical thresholds were $\Delta$NDVI $\leq-0.00543$, $\Delta$MNDWI $\leq-0.03743$ and $\Delta$NDBI $\geq0.00835$. Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} locates their convergence relative to the wider pressure pattern.

At 500 m, NDVI- and NDBI-pressure ranks were strongly aligned ($\rho=0.972$). Their grouping therefore affects how surface-cover information contributes to the hierarchy. Giri's surface-cover term was low (0.0176), while its MNDWI-pressure percentile was 0.9941 and terrain score 0.6765. Its leading composite priority consequently arose from the moisture-related spectral and terrain terms, rather than agreement among every indicator.

\subsection{Baselines and ablations identify the drivers of priority}
Giri complex led the fixed 500 m score (0.5912), followed by Giri Mosque and tombs (0.5725) and Jaulian (0.5676). The ungrouped equal-factor baseline retained only three of the reference top five, with $\rho=0.844$ and a maximum movement of seven ranks; it placed Bhallar first. Landscape-only scoring retained one of the top five ($\rho=0.325$; maximum movement 11) and selected Sirkap, whereas terrain-only scoring retained four ($\rho=0.830$; maximum movement seven) and selected Bhallar.

Factor ablations were markedly asymmetric (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a). Removing NDVI or NDBI retained $\rho=0.991$ and 0.990, respectively, and left Giri first. Removing MNDWI instead produced $\rho=-0.280$, no top-five overlap and a maximum movement of 13 positions. Removing slope or drainage proximity produced $\rho=0.611$ and 0.454 and placed Sirsukh first. The hierarchy thus reduced duplication between the vegetation and built/bare terms, while the moisture-related indicator and terrain pathways remained consequential to the decision.

\subsection{Spatial support, structural choices and inspection tiers}
Rank agreement was stronger between 500 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.918$) than between 250 and 1,000 m ($\rho=0.711$; Fig.~\ref{fig:scales}). Jaulian ranked 2, 3 and 1 at the three radii, whereas Giri complex ranked 4, 1 and 2. Giri Mosque and tombs moved from first at 250 m to fourth at 1,000 m.

Across the 27 structural scenarios, Giri complex, Giri Mosque and tombs, and Jaulian formed the core tier, appearing in the top three in 17, 20 and 18 scenarios, respectively. Khader Mohra and Mohra Moradu formed the conditional tier, appearing in the top five in 15 and 17 scenarios. Scenario agreement therefore supports a small inspection set more clearly than a unique ordering. Continuous domain-weight perturbation reinforced this distinction: Giri's top-three frequency increased from 0.683 to 0.869 as $\alpha$ increased from 0.5 to 2, concentrating weights towards an even balance. Bhallar's frequency decreased from 0.371 to 0.221.

Exact crossings sharpen this interpretation (Eq.~\ref{eq:crossing}). At 500 m, Giri leads for $0.1047<w<0.7692$: Bhallar leads below this interval, followed at higher weights by a narrow Kalawan interval and then Sirkap above 0.7857. At 250 m, leadership passes from Jaulian to Giri Mosque and tombs at 0.2941 and to Sirkap at 0.7306. At 1,000 m, Jaulian leads until 0.7143, followed briefly by Khader Mohra and then Dharmarajika. Thus a broad weight interval at one radius does not imply leadership across radii. Exact uniform-weight top-three frequencies agreed with the 50,000-draw experiment within 0.0024 across components.

\subsection{Spatial, positional and joint uncertainty}
Shared-block resampling gave Giri top-three frequencies of 0.994, 0.971 and 0.960 for 90, 150 and 300 m blocks (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b). Bhallar's frequencies were 0.914, 0.931 and 0.815, while Giri Mosque and tombs declined from 0.773 to 0.390. Larger blocks therefore altered uncertainty unevenly across components.

The undisplaced re-extraction control ranked Bhallar first, Giri second and Jaulian third, demonstrating a difference from the fixed summary-table ordering before positional perturbation. Relative to that control, displacement effects were modest for Bhallar and Giri: at 120 m their top-three frequencies were 1.000 and 0.993. Jaulian declined from 0.915 at 30 m to 0.824 at 120 m, while Giri Mosque and tombs increased from 0.086 to 0.180 (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}c). Feature reconstruction and positional uncertainty thus make separable contributions and should not be interpreted as the same experiment.

Joint simulation widened the decision envelope (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}d). Giri had median rank 2, interval 1--9 and top-three frequency 0.7324; Bhallar had median rank 3, interval 1--13 and frequency 0.5260. Giri Mosque and tombs and Jaulian had frequencies 0.4626 and 0.4096. In particular, Bhallar's prominence under spatial reconstruction contrasted with its limited top-three persistence across the fixed structural scenarios (2/27). This identifies a component requiring a mechanism-led drainage and terrain inspection, rather than treating one aggregate frequency as a universally stable priority.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition-matched conditions}
Open-Meteo annual precipitation decreased by 10.30 mm year$^{-1}$ (95\% residual-block interval $-17.91$ to $-2.67$; annual-family $q=0.027$), while annual mean temperature increased by 0.0418~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$ (0.0099--0.0731; $q=0.026$). Heavy-precipitation days declined by 0.143 days year$^{-1}$ ($q=0.018$). Soil moisture also declined, although these inferences remain conditional on the product and temporal dependence model.

NASA POWER gave opposing point slopes: +29.21 mm year$^{-1}$ for precipitation and $-0.0372$~$^\circ$C year$^{-1}$ for temperature. Annual precipitation agreement across 35 years was weak (Spearman $\rho=0.083$; RMSE 646.74 mm year$^{-1}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:weather}).

Acquisition-window matching further differentiated the epochs. E2024 antecedent precipitation averaged 42.47, 221.19 and 852.92 mm over 30, 90 and 365 days. The maxima occurred instead in E2004, E2019 and E2014, respectively. Thus the weather preceding the retained satellite scenes cannot be represented reliably by assigning a single wet or dry label to an epoch. Detailed event definitions and annual trends are supplied in Supplementary Table~S2.

\subsection{Seven-family spatial comparison and diagnostic experiments}
Buffered development validation selected \ExtensionModel{} with mean macro-F1 \ExtensionCV{}. On the 4,188 outer-stripe samples, it achieved macro-F1 \ExtensionFOne{} (95\% test-block interval \ExtensionLow{}--\ExtensionHigh{}), agreement \ExtensionAgreement{}, log loss 0.498 and calibration error 0.014 (Table~\ref{tab:models}). The MLP achieved the largest outer macro-F1 (0.849), but its lower development score did not support selecting it as the primary model.

Paired spatial resampling showed random forest exceeding logistic regression by 0.0145 macro-F1 (95\% interval 0.0028--0.0283). Histogram gradient boosting exceeded random forest by 0.0248 (0.0117--0.0663), whereas the MLP comparison had an interval spanning zero. These unadjusted comparisons distinguish development selection from outer-stripe performance.

The feature controls provide a complementary interpretation (Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics}). Fixed-capacity logistic regression using bands alone reached macro-F1 0.824, whereas indices alone reached 0.671. Location-only, shuffled-label and class-prior controls achieved 0.140, 0.070 and 0.049. Spectral information therefore explained substantially more agreement than coordinates or these null controls, but the selected ten-feature random forest did not outperform the simpler bands-only control by its point estimate.

Development-only temperature scaling ($\tau=1.658$) increased outer log loss from 0.498 to 0.574, Brier score from 0.269 to 0.300 and calibration error from 0.014 to 0.136. The calibration learned from geographically separated folds transferred poorly to the outer stripe. Random-forest macro-F1 ranged only from 0.8205 to 0.8214 across three refit seeds and matched between Colab and Windows, indicating that the larger differences arose from modelling and spatial-evaluation choices rather than the selected model's refit seed.
