\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What the framework measures, and what it does not}
The comparison with independent evidence (Table~\ref{tab:external}) most
constrains how this framework should be used. None of the five Taxila components
with independently published damage or encroachment falls within the CHIP core
tier, and the component with the clearest documented loss of designated area
cannot be scored at all. Either the framework is failing at a task it was built for, or it is succeeding
at a different task from the one the published evidence records, and the internal
evidence supports the second reading. Removing MNDWI destroys the ordering while
removing the vegetation or built/bare terms leaves it essentially unchanged, so
the ordering is driven by moisture-related and terrain-hydrological evidence,
whereas Khan et al. document fabric damage and seismic amplification
\citep{khan2022taxila} and Butt et al. built-up clustering and core-zone
reduction \citep{butt2025taxila}. These are different constructs and need not
coincide.

CHIP is therefore a screening layer for moisture- and drainage-driven landscape
pressure, to be combined with damage and encroachment records rather than
substituted for them. A defensible Taxila programme would visit the four
components the budget analysis identifies, and separately those already on record
with independent authorities. Presenting a single environmental ranking as a
conservation priority list would have directed inspection away from Bhir Mound
and Saraikala; this analysis makes that failure mode visible before the ranking
is used.

\subsection{From environmental indicators to inspection decisions}
Within its construct, the framework's contribution is that a score travels with
evidence about what would change it, in three ways a frequency summary cannot
express.

Confidence factors show that no component is a confident first priority: the
largest is 0.42, so even at its most favourable weighting the best-supported
component loses the lead in most spatial states.

The variance decomposition converts sensitivity analysis into a work
instruction. A rank dominated by the landscape weight cannot be settled by better
data, and one dominated by spatial support cannot be settled by deliberation.
Both groups are substantial at Taxila and individually identified, so a manager
can act on the diagnosis: convene a weighting decision for Giri Mosque and tombs,
Sirkap and Giri complex; commission better geometry for Kalawan, Khanpur Cave and
Pippala; and resolve the terrain formulation for Bhallar.

The budget curve prices the recommendation. Three components cover a scenario's
own three highest priorities in fewer than one case in five; a fourth more than
doubles that. Reporting a core tier without its coverage probability invites an
authority to treat three visits as sufficient when the framework's own
uncertainty does not support it. The central weights add that the property
separates into terrain-led and land-cover-led priorities, so which group is
visited first follows from one declared parameter. Bhallar shows why these
distributions must retain their definitions: seldom in the fixed structural top
three (2/27) yet prominent after re-extraction, it holds both the highest
first-rank acceptability (0.310) and the largest terrain-formulation variance
share (29.3\%).

These results complement Taxila research combining ground and satellite evidence
\citep{khan2022taxila}, wider heritage multi-hazard assessment
\citep{guerriero2022derwent,romao2022risk,yang2023yongtai} and recent South Asian
World Heritage assessment \citep{megarry2026landcover}.

\subsection{Limitations}
The most consequential limitation is the exclusion of Saraikala. One of the
eighteen inscribed components carries no usable coordinate in the records
available to us, so it is absent from every experiment reported here, and it is
the component for which evidence of designated-area loss is strongest
\citep{butt2025taxila}. A framework that cannot score a component cannot
recommend inspecting it. Resolving Saraikala's geometry, ideally as a polygon, is
the highest-value data improvement for this property, and inventory completeness
should be a precondition for prioritisation rather than a caveat on it.

Point-centred neighbourhoods approximate landscape context rather than monument
footprints, so the central weights and variance shares are conditional on that
support. Percentile scores depend on the analysed inventory, D8 routing cannot
resolve local walls or culverts, and the simulations quantify only the stated
perturbations. The affine reconstruction is exact but inherits the recorded
design: 34 of 500 spatial states were not identifiable and were excluded, and the
retained 466 are not a random subsample. The five externally documented
components are a convenience sample using heterogeneous methods, so the absence
of overlap with the core tier indicates construct separation but does not
quantify accuracy against condition. Opposing product trends also prevent
attributing the landscape pattern to regional drying or warming
\citep{sesana2021climate,daly2022adaptation}.

The next validation should use independently assessed component-condition
observations across all inscribed components, record assessor agreement, and test
whether environmental screening of this kind adds information beyond what damage
and encroachment records already supply.
