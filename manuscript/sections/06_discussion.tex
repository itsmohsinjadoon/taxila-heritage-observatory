\section{Discussion}
\subsection{From environmental indicators to inspection decisions}
The principal contribution is the experimental evaluation of an inspection rule across several distinct sources of uncertainty. A component's score is accompanied by evidence about its drivers, its persistence across support and structural choices, and its response to spatial reconstruction. This extends the practical value of a priority map: an inspection team can identify both where to visit and which environmental explanation requires verification.

The ablations show why the hierarchy matters. NDVI and NDBI supplied closely aligned information, while MNDWI and terrain pathways strongly affected the ordering. Giri's leading score was consequently associated with moisture-related spectral and terrain terms despite low surface-cover pressure. An appropriate field visit would investigate drainage routes, surface moisture and their relationship to the monument fabric, while a landscape-led priority such as Sirkap would motivate examination of surrounding surface-cover change. These are testable inspection questions rather than interchangeable meanings of a high score.

The results complement Taxila research combining ground observations and satellite evidence \citep{khan2022taxila}, and wider heritage multi-hazard assessments \citep{guerriero2022derwent,romao2022risk}. CHIP adds a structured way to test how priorities respond to analytical choices before those priorities are operationalised. The transferable contribution is the experimental procedure; the Taxila percentiles, weights and tier thresholds require local reassessment at another property.

\subsection{Interpreting persistence and disagreement}
Giri, Giri Mosque and tombs, and Jaulian persisted across the structural scenarios, supporting a core inspection set. However, Bhallar illustrates why scenario frequencies must retain their definitions. It was seldom in the fixed structural top three but prominent after feature re-extraction and under spatial simulation. Combining those results into one unconditional confidence score would conceal the information most relevant to planning: Bhallar's priority depends strongly on how its local terrain and spectral support are represented.

Joint intervals are wider than the certainty implied by a single ranking. They justify combining a persistent core set with additional visits targeted to particular mechanisms and representation-sensitive components. Access, cultural significance, repair history and specialist assessment would then guide the operational sequence. Those management factors should enter as explicit decisions rather than being inferred from remotely sensed variables.

\subsection{What the predictive experiments establish}
The seven-family comparison demonstrates useful geographic land-cover agreement, while the controls reveal where extra complexity adds little. Bands-only logistic regression remained competitive with the selected random forest, and temperature scaling worsened probability quality. Development selection and outer performance also diverged. Reporting these experiments together is more informative for model choice than selecting the largest test score: the appropriate model depends on spatial transfer, uncertainty and the intended output, not only point accuracy.

These findings are consistent with the importance of spatial evaluation and applicability domains \citep{hijmans2012cv,meyer2018target,meyer2021aoa}. Recent tabular foundation models offer additional comparative opportunities \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn}, but the present target remains consensus land cover. Class-stratified sampling, temporal mismatch and geographically concentrated water labels constrain interpretation. The previously examined outer stripe makes this a retrospective comparison, and neither it nor a more capable classifier provides independent monument-condition validation.

\subsection{Climate interpretation and study limitations}
Opposing product trends prevent a simple attribution of the landscape pattern to regional drying or warming. Acquisition-matched weather describes conditions relevant to the observations more directly, but five composite epochs provide limited temporal replication. Station measurements, soil observations and higher-frequency imagery would help distinguish climatic responses from management, seasonality and land-use processes \citep{sesana2021climate,daly2022adaptation}.

Point-centred neighbourhoods approximate landscape context rather than monument footprints; Saraikala lacks usable geometry. DEM routing cannot resolve every local obstruction, and percentile scores depend on the analysed inventory. Spatial simulations quantify stated perturbations without covering all label, sensor or conservation-model error. The next validation should therefore use independently assessed component-condition observations, record assessor agreement and evaluate components or periods excluded from design choices. Such evidence would test whether the proposed inspection strategy identifies actionable conservation problems.
