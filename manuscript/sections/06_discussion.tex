\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What the framework measures, and what it does not}
Independent evidence (Table~\ref{tab:external}) limits the framework's interpretation. None of five components with published damage or encroachment falls in CHIP's structural core tier, and Saraikala, with documented core-zone loss, cannot be scored. Ablation shows why: removing MNDWI disrupts the order, while removing vegetation or built/bare terms barely changes it. CHIP thus responds chiefly to moisture-related and terrain-hydrological evidence, whereas Khan et al. report fabric damage and seismic amplification \citep{khan2022taxila} and Butt et al. built-up clustering and core-zone reduction \citep{butt2025taxila}. These constructs need not coincide.

CHIP should therefore screen moisture- and drainage-driven landscape pressure alongside damage and encroachment records. A Taxila programme would inspect the four components selected by the budget analysis and separately those documented by other authorities. Treating the environmental order as a general condition ranking would miss Bhir Mound and Saraikala.

\subsection{From environmental indicators to inspection decisions}

Confidence factors show that no component is a confident first priority: the
largest is 0.42, so even at its most favourable weighting the best-supported
component loses the lead in most spatial states.

Variance decomposition identifies which assumption to resolve: better data cannot settle a weight-driven rank, and deliberation cannot settle a support-driven rank. At Taxila, weighting decisions matter most for Giri Mosque and tombs, Sirkap and Giri complex; better geometry matters for Kalawan, Khanpur Cave and Pippala; terrain formulation matters for Bhallar.

Three visits cover a scenario's three highest priorities in fewer than one case in five; a fourth more than doubles coverage. A structural core tier alone could therefore understate the required inspection budget. Central weights distinguish terrain-led from land-cover-led priorities. Bhallar illustrates why the uncertainty designs must stay separate: it appeared in the fixed structural top three only 2/27 times, yet after re-extraction had the highest first-rank acceptability (0.310) and the largest terrain-formulation variance share (29.3\%).

These findings extend Taxila and wider heritage risk studies \citep{khan2022taxila,guerriero2022derwent,romao2022risk,yang2023yongtai,megarry2026landcover}.

\subsection{What the predictive experiments establish}
The seven-family comparison shows useful geographic land-cover agreement while
the controls reveal where complexity adds little: bands-only logistic regression
remained competitive, four families exceeded the selected model on the outer
stripe, and temperature scaling worsened probability quality. Read alongside
Table~\ref{tab:external}, the experiment functions as a negative control---the
spectral evidence supports land-cover discrimination, and such agreement says
nothing about monument condition
\citep{hijmans2012cv,meyer2018target,meyer2021aoa,ploton2020spatial}.

\subsection{Limitations}
The most consequential limitation is the exclusion of Saraikala. One of the
eighteen inscribed components carries no usable coordinate in the records
available to us, so it is absent from every experiment reported here, and it is
the component for which evidence of designated-area loss is strongest
\citep{butt2025taxila}. A framework that cannot score a component cannot
recommend inspecting it. Resolving Saraikala's geometry, ideally as a polygon, is
the highest-value data improvement for this property, and inventory completeness
should be a precondition for prioritisation rather than a caveat on it.

Point-centred neighbourhoods approximate landscape context rather than monument
footprints, so the central weights and variance shares are conditional on that
support. Percentile scores depend on the analysed inventory, D8 routing cannot
resolve local walls or culverts, and the simulations quantify only the stated
perturbations. The affine reconstruction is exact but inherits the recorded
design: 34 of 500 spatial states were not identifiable and were excluded, and the
retained 466 are not a random subsample. The five externally documented
components are a convenience sample using heterogeneous methods, so the absence
of overlap with the core tier indicates construct separation but does not
quantify accuracy against condition. Opposing product trends also prevent
attributing the landscape pattern to regional drying or warming
\citep{sesana2021climate,daly2022adaptation}.

The next validation should use independently assessed component-condition
observations across all inscribed components, record assessor agreement, and test
whether environmental screening of this kind adds information beyond what damage
and encroachment records already supply.
