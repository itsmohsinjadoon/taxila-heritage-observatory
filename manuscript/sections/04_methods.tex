\section{Materials and methods}
\subsection{Study area, inventory and evidence sources}
Taxila is a serial World Heritage property in Punjab, Pakistan, inscribed under reference 139 \citep{unesco_taxila}. Of its 18 component records, 17 carry usable point coordinates; Saraikala (139-002) is excluded from coordinate-dependent analysis because its location is unresolved. Spectral neighbourhoods used WGS 84/UTM zone 43N (EPSG:32643). Point-centred circles of 250, 500 and 1,000 m defined analytical neighbourhoods, 500 m for primary reporting; these are neither official property polygons nor statutory or UNESCO buffer zones (Fig.~\ref{fig:study}).

Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance supplied five post-monsoon three-year composites (2003--2005 to 2023--2025, abbreviated E2004--E2024) containing 11, 10, 8, 8 and 18 retained scenes from Landsat 7, 8 and 8/9 respectively. Source records preserve acquisition dates, sensor identities and quality information, and cross-sensor and masking limitations were considered explicitly \citep{usgs_landsat_c2,roy2016landsat}.

Daily Open-Meteo ERA5-Seamless data covered 1991--2025 \citep{openmeteo_archive,hersbach2020era5}, resolving to four unique weather cells of 12,784 daily records each; NASA POWER supplied a comparison product over the same period \citep{nasa_power}. Neither was treated as station truth. Terrain came from the one-arc-second (approximately 30~m) SRTM-derived N33E072 elevation tile distributed through AWS Terrain Tiles \citep{farr2007srtm}. Table~\ref{tab:data} summarises the role of each source. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} separates the paths through which these inputs inform interpretation.

\subsection{Spectral transformations and common-support convergence}
Cloud, shadow and invalid observations were masked using the Collection~2 quality assessment band, whose cloud and cloud-shadow logic derives from the Fmask algorithm \citep{zhu2012fmask,zhu2015fmask}, before compositing on a fixed 30 m grid. With scaled reflectance $\rho_b$ in band $b$, the indices were
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indices}
\mathrm{NDVI}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}-\rho_{\mathrm{red}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}+\rho_{\mathrm{red}}},\quad
\mathrm{NDBI}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{SWIR1}}-\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{SWIR1}}+\rho_{\mathrm{NIR}}},\quad
\mathrm{MNDWI}=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{green}}-\rho_{\mathrm{SWIR1}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{green}}+\rho_{\mathrm{SWIR1}}}.
\end{equation}
These ratios follow their original definitions \citep{tucker1979ndvi,zha2003ndbi,xu2006mndwi}. NDBI responds to bare soil as well as built surfaces; its adverse direction is a screening assumption, not an attribution of urban expansion.

For component $i$, epoch $e$, radius $r$ and factor $k$, let $x_{ierk}$ denote the neighbourhood median. The oriented empirical percentile is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:percentile}
z_{ierk}=\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal I_r}(s_kx_{ierk})-\tfrac12}{N_r},
\qquad s_N=s_W=-1,\quad s_B=+1.
\end{equation}
Ranks ascend with average ties; $N_r=85$ component--epoch observations at each radius ($17\times5$). Terrain percentiles use the 17 static summaries, while reconstruction experiments rerank the 17 simultaneously reconstructed E2024 summaries, so reconstruction differences include a reference-population effect. Percentiles describe relative evidence within their stated population, not transferable physical thresholds.

Endpoint differences $\Delta I_k(p)=I_{k,\mathrm{E2024}}(p)-I_{k,\mathrm{E2004}}(p)$ used only the common valid-cell set $\Omega$. With $Q_k(q)$ denoting its empirical $q$-quantile,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convergence}
\begin{aligned}
C(p)&=\mathbf1[\Delta I_N(p)\leq Q_N(.2)]+\mathbf1[\Delta I_W(p)\leq Q_W(.2)]
       +\mathbf1[\Delta I_B(p)\geq Q_B(.8)],\\
\pi_{\geq h}&=|\Omega|^{-1}\sum_{p\in\Omega}\mathbf1[C(p)\geq h],\qquad h\in\{2,3\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The indicator function $\mathbf1[\cdot]$ equals one when its condition holds. These overlapping, data-derived tails quantify descriptive convergence; they are neither independent tests nor measurements of damaged area.

\subsection{Terrain representation and hierarchical decision rule}
Single-direction (D8) routing directs each elevation cell to its steepest strictly lower neighbour; multiple-direction schemes are more realistic on divergent terrain \citep{quinn1991flow,tarboton1997flow} but D8 suffices because only a relative, percentile-transformed accumulation summary is used. Slope $\theta$ derives from metric elevation gradients. The contributing-cell count $A_c$ and geometric-mean cell width $h$ define the numerical wetness proxy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wetness}
H(p)=\log\!\left[\frac{(A_c(p)+1)\,h/(1\,\mathrm{m})}
 {\tan(\max\{\theta(p),0.05^\circ\})+0.01}\right].
\end{equation}
follows the contributing-area/slope rationale of hydrological terrain analysis \citep{beven1979topmodel,moore1991terrain}. Drainage comprises cells at or above the 99th percentile of accumulation, and component factors are the 90th percentiles of slope and $H$ and the median Euclidean drainage distance, oriented so that higher slope and wetness and shorter distance increase susceptibility. Routing approximates topographic convergence, not discharge.

With $S,H,D$ indexing slope, wetness and drainage proximity, landscape pressure $L$, terrain susceptibility $T$ and priority $P$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:priority}
\begin{aligned}
L_{ier}&=\tfrac14z_{ierN}+\tfrac14z_{ierB}+\tfrac12z_{ierW},\\
T_{ir}&=\tfrac13(z_{irS}+z_{irH}+z_{irD}),\\
P_{ier}(w)&=wL_{ier}+(1-w)T_{ir},\qquad P_{ier}^{\mathrm{ref}}=P_{ier}(0.5).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Grouping NDVI and NDBI limits their combined weight relative to MNDWI without claiming statistical independence, and the equal domain weights are declared choices rather than fitted conservation parameters. The construction connects GIS multicriteria assessment \citep{malczewski2006gis} to testable sensitivity assumptions \citep{chen2010sensitivity,feizizadeh2014uncertainty}. A regional climate term stays contextual: adding the same value to every component is an affine transformation of $P^{\mathrm{ref}}$ and cannot alter the within-epoch ordering.

\subsection{Structural experiments and exact weight transitions}
The hierarchy was compared with seven alternative aggregation rules: an equal
six-factor mean; landscape-only and terrain-only restrictions; CRITIC and entropy
objective weighting; a robust-$z$ logistic transform; and the oriented first
principal component. Factor ablations removed one term and renormalised the rest.
Spearman correlation, top-five overlap and maximum rank displacement describe
ordering changes, and because every comparison uses $n=17$ components each
coefficient carries a 95\% Bonett--Wright interval; point coefficients on 17 units
are not separable without them.

Three radii, three landscape weights ($0.25,0.50,0.75$) and three terrain formulations produce 27 scenarios; the formulations are equal factors, inverse-correlation weights ($a_k\propto[\sum_\ell|\rho_{k\ell}|]^{-1}$, $\sum_ka_k=1$, from the Spearman matrix of the three terrain factors including its diagonal) and the reduced slope--drainage mean. Continuous-weight experiments sample $w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\alpha)$ for $\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, 50,000 draws each, evaluating stated preference distributions rather than inferring stakeholders' preferences \citep{lahdelma2001smaa}.

An additional deterministic analysis resolves every pairwise score crossing. For E2024 components $i,j$ at fixed radius and equal-factor terrain, solving $P_i(w)=P_j(w)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossing}
w^*_{ij}=\frac{T_j-T_i}{(L_i-T_i)-(L_j-T_j)}.
\end{equation}
Retaining nonzero denominators and crossings in $(0,1)$ and sorting them partitions the weight interval into regions of constant rank order, with midpoint evaluation identifying each leader. Figure~\ref{fig:scales} combines fixed ranks, a three-dimensional rank response and exact leadership intervals; radii remain discrete.

\subsection{Spatial, positional and joint uncertainty propagation}
Three perturbation designs precede the reconstruction: shared-block resampling (2,000 spectral realisations at each of 90, 150 and 300~m, using exponential block weights shared across overlapping neighbourhoods and a weighted median); coordinate displacement (spectral and terrain re-extraction at 30, 60 and 120~m offsets against an undisplaced control); and the joint simulation (500 spatial states, each under ten decision settings sampling $w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$ and a terrain formulation, giving 5,000 draws). These are conditional sensitivity distributions over declared perturbations, not posterior probabilities of deterioration. Full definitions in Supplementary Section~S6.

\subsection{Deterministic reconstruction of the joint design}
Equation~\eqref{eq:priority} is affine in $w$, so the recorded score for spatial
state $s$ and terrain formulation $m$ satisfies
$P_i(s,m,w)=wL_i(s)+(1-w)T_i(s,m)$, and the latent terms are identified by the
recorded draws. Stacking the ten decision settings of each state and solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
\begin{bmatrix}P_i^{(1)}\\ \vdots\\ P_i^{(10)}\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
w^{(1)} & (1-w^{(1)})\mathbf e_{m^{(1)}}^{\top}\\
\vdots & \vdots\\
w^{(10)} & (1-w^{(10)})\mathbf e_{m^{(10)}}^{\top}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}L_i(s)\\ \mathbf T_i(s)\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
in the least-squares sense recovers $L_i(s)$ and the three terrain terms
$\mathbf T_i(s)$ exactly, where $\mathbf e_m$ selects formulation $m$. The system
is noiseless; the maximum residual across all states and components was
$6.6\times10^{-15}$. A state is retained when the design matrix has full rank and
all three formulations appear, which held for 466 of 500 states.

Recovery replaces the 5,000 draws with a balanced full factorial over those
states, three formulations and a uniform grid $w\in\{0,0.005,\dots,1\}$: 280,998
scenarios without simulation error. Writing $R_i(s,m,w)$ for the rank, the SMAA
central weight and confidence factor \citep{lahdelma2001smaa} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central}
w_i^{c}=\mathbb E\!\left[w \mid R_i=1\right],\qquad
p_i^{c}=\Pr\!\left(R_i=1 \mid w=w_i^{c}\right),
\end{equation}
the latter evaluated at the grid point nearest $w_i^{c}$. Treating spatial state
and the decision pair $(m,w)$ as the two factors of the balanced design, rank
variance partitions as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vardecomp}
\operatorname{Var}(R_i)=\underbrace{V_i^{S}}_{\text{spatial support}}
+\underbrace{V_i^{D}}_{\text{decision}}
+\underbrace{V_i^{SD}}_{\text{interaction}},
\end{equation}
with main-effect sums of squares formed from the marginal means
\citep{helton2006sensitivity} and the decision term split further into
landscape-weight and terrain-formulation shares. These state, per component,
whether resolving a preference or improving the spatial support would do more to
settle its rank.

For a programme visiting the $k$ highest-priority components $A_k$, and writing
$\mathcal T(s,m,w)$ for the three highest-scoring components in a scenario, the
expected shortfall and coverage probability are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage}
\mathbb E\!\left[\left|\mathcal T\setminus A_k\right|\right],\qquad
\Pr\!\left(\mathcal T\subseteq A_k\right),
\end{equation}
evaluated over the full factorial. These convert the rank distribution into a
budget statement: how many components must be visited before the programme is
likely to contain the priorities the framework itself identifies.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition-matched forcing}
The regional daily series averages the four unique weather cells equally before annual aggregation. Annual trends use the Theil--Sen estimator with 2,000 five-year residual-block replicates and Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment across 12 annual metrics \citep{sen1968slope,yue2002autocorrelation,benjamini1995fdr}. Acquisition-matched forcing averages antecedent precipitation and temperature over 7, 30, 90, 180 and 365~day windows preceding each retained scene date. Inference is conditional on the weather product; five composite epochs provide only five temporal replicates. Full definitions in Supplementary Section~S5.

\subsection{Computational implementation}
Model experiments ran in Google Colab and independently on Windows with recorded package versions, spatial splits, configurations and per-sample probabilities. Derived inputs and deterministic figure/table builders support reproduction \citep{wilson2017practices,nust2018reproducible}. Scene manifests document acquisition and preprocessing. The exact weight analysis uses recorded component summaries without refitting their percentile transforms.
