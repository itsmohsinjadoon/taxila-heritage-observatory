\section{Materials and methods}
\subsection{Study area, inventory and evidence sources}
Taxila is a serial World Heritage property in Punjab, Pakistan, inscribed under reference 139 \citep{unesco_taxila}. The inventory contained 18 component records. Seventeen had usable point coordinates; Saraikala (139-002) remained in the documentary inventory but was excluded from coordinate-dependent analysis because its location was unresolved. All metric operations used WGS 84/UTM zone 43N (EPSG:32643). Point-centred circles of 250, 500 and 1,000 m defined analytical neighbourhoods, with 500 m used for primary reporting. They are neither official property polygons nor statutory or UNESCO buffer zones (Fig.~\ref{fig:study}).

Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance supplied five post-monsoon three-year composites: 2003--2005, 2008--2010, 2013--2015, 2018--2020 and 2023--2025, abbreviated E2004 to E2024. They contained 11, 10, 8, 8 and 18 retained scenes, respectively. Landsat 7 supplied the first two epochs, Landsat 8 the middle two, and Landsat 8/9 the final epoch. Source records preserve acquisition dates, sensor identities and quality information. Cross-sensor and masking limitations were considered explicitly \citep{usgs_landsat_c2,roy2016landsat}.

Daily Open-Meteo ERA5-Seamless data covered 1991--2025 \citep{openmeteo_archive}. Component queries resolved to four unique weather cells, each containing 12,784 daily records. NASA POWER supplied a comparison product over the same period \citep{nasa_power}. Neither was treated as station truth. Terrain came from the one-arc-second N33E072 elevation tile distributed through AWS Terrain Tiles. ESA WorldCover 2020/2021 consensus \citep{esa_worldcover} supplied six proxy land-cover classes for an E2019 feature table. Table~\ref{tab:data} summarises the role of each source. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} separates the paths through which these inputs inform interpretation.

\subsection{Comparable spectral evidence}
Cloud, shadow and invalid observations were masked before compositing on a fixed 30 m grid. Endpoint change used the intersection of valid E2004 and E2024 cells, preserving a common denominator. NDVI, NDBI and MNDWI were calculated from scaled reflectance using their standard band ratios \citep{zha2003ndbi,xu2006mndwi}. Lower NDVI and MNDWI and higher NDBI were oriented towards greater relative pressure. NDBI can respond to bare soil as well as built surfaces; its direction was a screening assumption, not an attribution of urban expansion.

Within each radius, component-epoch medians were transformed to empirical percentiles using the pooled component-epoch distribution. Denote the oriented percentiles by $z_N,z_B,z_W$. Correlated vegetation and built/bare terms were grouped before combination:
\begin{equation}
L=\tfrac12(\tfrac12z_N+\tfrac12z_B)+\tfrac12z_W.
\end{equation}
This structure reduces the influence that two closely related indicators would otherwise receive in an equal three-term sum. It does not remove all statistical dependence. Percentiles are conditional on the analysed inventory and epochs; they are not transferable physical thresholds.

For endpoint changes, the lower 20\% tails of NDVI and MNDWI and the upper 20\% tail of NDBI defined three adverse criteria. Their sum identified cells meeting at least two or all three criteria. Because these thresholds are data-derived and the indicators correlated, convergence measures descriptive screening prevalence rather than damaged area or statistical hotspot significance. Scene provenance and preprocessing specifications accompany the analysis.

\subsection{Terrain, priority and uncertainty}
Slope, a contributing-area wetness proxy and distance to a thresholded D8 drainage network described terrain context \citep{farr2007srtm,beven1979topmodel}. Higher slope and wetness and shorter drainage distance were oriented towards greater susceptibility. Their component percentiles were averaged to obtain $T$. Flow accumulation represents topographic convergence, not discharge; the DEM cannot resolve all culverts, walls or local drains.

The primary score was $P=0.5L+0.5T$. Equal domain weights were a declared structural choice, not an expert-elicited preference or fitted conservation model. Climate was not used to create fine-scale rank differences. A separate integrated context score was retained as a sensitivity analysis. Scores are dimensionless and relative to the same 17 mapped components.

\subsection{Experimental assessment of inspection priorities}
The experiments examine complementary aspects of the decision rule. First, the hierarchical score was compared with an ungrouped equal-factor mean and landscape-only and terrain-only baselines. Leave-one-factor-out scores were recomputed with retained weights renormalised. Spearman correlation, Kendall concordance, top-five overlap and maximum rank movement quantify departures from the reference; the component ranked first identifies their management consequence.

Second, the 250, 500 and 1,000 m neighbourhoods were crossed with landscape weights of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75 and three terrain formulations: equal factors, inverse-correlation-weighted factors, and a reduced slope--drainage formulation. The resulting 27 scenarios assess structural persistence. Components occurring in the top three in at least half the scenarios define a core inspection tier; components outside that tier occurring in the top five in at least half define a conditional tier. These operational thresholds organise scenario frequencies and do not represent measured damage severity. A continuous weight experiment sampled $w\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\alpha)$ in $P_w=wL+(1-w)T$, with $\alpha=0.5,1,2$ and 50,000 draws per setting.

Third, 2,000 spatial draws were generated at each of 90, 150 and 300 m block sizes within the 500 m neighbourhoods. Exponential block weights were shared across components occupying the same grid blocks and multiplied by valid-cell counts. Support-weighted medians of block-level spectral medians supplied each draw, with terrain fixed. This design represents spatial dependence and overlap between analytical neighbourhoods.

Fourth, component centres were displaced independently within uniform-area discs of maximum radius 30, 60 or 120 m. Spectral and terrain summaries were re-extracted and percentiles recalculated for 2,000 draws per radius. An undisplaced re-extraction provides the control. It is distinguished from the fixed summary-table reference because feature reconstruction itself can change ranks; differences between these controls must not be attributed to positional error.

Finally, 500 spatial states combined a sampled block size, shared block weights and independently displaced centres within 120 m. Each state was crossed with ten decision settings, sampling a uniform landscape weight and one of the three terrain formulations. The resulting 5,000 score vectors quantify joint sensitivity; they are nested simulations, not 5,000 independent geographic observations. Median ranks, central 95\% intervals and top-three frequencies summarise the conditional distributions. Figures~\ref{fig:scales} and \ref{fig:robustness} report the central spatial, structural and joint experiments in the main article.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition windows}
The regional series averages each day equally across the four unique weather cells before annual aggregation; it is not an average over 17 independent component climates. Annual metrics were characterised by Theil--Sen slopes \citep{sen1968slope}. For annual uncertainty analysis, we fitted a robust linear trend, resampled five-year blocks of its residuals and restored the fitted trend to obtain slope intervals. Null slopes were calculated by adding the same resampled residuals to a constant baseline. The two-sided Monte Carlo probability was $(1+\#\{|\beta_b^0|\geq|\widehat\beta|\})/(B+1)$, with $B=2,000$. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment was applied to the 12 annual metrics \citep{benjamini1995fdr}. The procedure assumes that detrended residual blocks adequately represent short-range dependence; it does not resolve product bias.

Antecedent weather was calculated for every Landsat acquisition over 7, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days and then averaged within each epoch. Product comparisons used matched dates and periods. Weather--spectral correlations involved only five independent composite epochs and were retained as exploratory, without causal interpretation. No weather field was downscaled to the Landsat grid.

\subsection{Retrospective spatial proxy benchmark}
The feature table contained 20,749 class-stratified samples with six reflectance bands and four indices. The outer split assigned 4,188 samples in block columns 3--4 to testing, excluded 4,467 samples in columns 2 and 5 as a buffer, and retained 12,094 development samples. The 20 test blocks did not overlap the 60 development blocks. The minimum observed sample separation was calculated from grid coordinates. Labels represent WorldCover agreement and carry temporal mismatch with the E2019 predictors.

The experiment compared multinomial logistic regression, random forest, extra trees, histogram gradient boosting, a multilayer perceptron, XGBoost and CatBoost \citep{breiman2001rf,pedregosa2011sklearn,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost}. Each family received three predeclared configurations and the same three contiguous inner validation bands. Adjacent block rows were excluded from inner training. Standardisation was fitted inside each training fold; early stopping was disabled to avoid an implicit random validation split. Mean inner macro-F1, computed over all six classes even when a fold omitted a class, selected the primary family and configuration before outer predictions were generated.

The water proxy was geographically concentrated and absent from one inner training fold. We retained this difficult fold, assigned zero predicted mass to an unseen class where necessary, and kept the six-class scoring denominator. This tests transfer under the observed label geography rather than manufacturing class coverage. It also limits the precision of comparisons.

Outer metrics included macro-F1, fixed-six-class mean recall, overall agreement, log loss, Brier score and ten-bin top-label calibration error. Two thousand shared test-block resamples produced paired macro-F1 differences and percentile intervals. A scalar temperature was estimated from development out-of-fold predictions and applied to outer probabilities. Fixed-capacity bands-only, indices-only, location-only, shuffled-label and class-prior controls diagnosed information content; three seeds assessed the selected model's refit sensitivity. These controls did not alter primary selection.

The outer stripe had been inspected in earlier analyses. The comparison is therefore retrospective, even though its model selection uses development data only. It does not provide a new untouched test, a population-area accuracy estimate, or independent evidence of monument condition \citep{olofsson2014accuracy,foody2002accuracy}.

\subsection{Computational implementation}
The land-cover experiments were executed in Google Colab and independently repeated on Windows with recorded package versions. Model selection was completed before outer predictions were generated. Colab outputs supply the main model comparison. The accompanying code records input identifiers, spatial splits, configurations and per-sample probabilities, supporting reproduction from the distributed derived-data package \citep{wilson2017practices,nust2018reproducible}. Source acquisition and preprocessing are documented separately through scene manifests and provider specifications.
