\section{Materials and methods}
\subsection{Study area, inventory and evidence sources}
Taxila is a serial World Heritage property in Punjab, Pakistan, inscribed under reference 139 \citep{unesco_taxila}. The inventory contained 18 component records. Seventeen had usable point coordinates; Saraikala (139-002) remained in the documentary inventory but was excluded from coordinate-dependent analysis because its location was unresolved. All metric operations used WGS 84/UTM zone 43N (EPSG:32643). Point-centred circles of 250, 500 and 1,000 m defined analytical neighbourhoods, with 500 m used for primary reporting. They are neither official property polygons nor statutory or UNESCO buffer zones (Fig.~\ref{fig:study}).

Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance supplied five post-monsoon three-year composites: 2003--2005, 2008--2010, 2013--2015, 2018--2020 and 2023--2025, abbreviated E2004 to E2024. They contained 11, 10, 8, 8 and 18 retained scenes, respectively. Landsat 7 supplied the first two epochs, Landsat 8 the middle two, and Landsat 8/9 the final epoch. Source records preserve acquisition dates, sensor identities and quality information. Cross-sensor and masking limitations were considered explicitly \citep{usgs_landsat_c2,roy2016landsat}.

Daily Open-Meteo ERA5-Seamless data covered 1991--2025 \citep{openmeteo_archive}. Component queries resolved to four unique weather cells, each containing 12,784 daily records. NASA POWER supplied a comparison product over the same period \citep{nasa_power}. Neither was treated as station truth. Terrain came from the one-arc-second N33E072 elevation tile distributed through AWS Terrain Tiles. ESA WorldCover 2020/2021 consensus \citep{esa_worldcover} supplied six proxy land-cover classes for an E2019 feature table. Table~\ref{tab:data} summarises the role of each source. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} separates the paths through which these inputs inform interpretation.

\subsection{Comparable spectral evidence}
Cloud, shadow and invalid observations were masked before compositing on a fixed 30 m grid. Endpoint change used the intersection of valid E2004 and E2024 cells, preserving a common denominator. NDVI, NDBI and MNDWI were calculated from scaled reflectance using their standard band ratios \citep{zha2003ndbi,xu2006mndwi}. Lower NDVI and MNDWI and higher NDBI were oriented towards greater relative pressure. NDBI can respond to bare soil as well as built surfaces; its direction was a screening assumption, not an attribution of urban expansion.

Within each radius, component-epoch medians were transformed to empirical percentiles using the pooled component-epoch distribution. Denote the oriented percentiles by $z_N,z_B,z_W$. Correlated vegetation and built/bare terms were grouped before combination:
\begin{equation}
L=\tfrac12(\tfrac12z_N+\tfrac12z_B)+\tfrac12z_W.
\end{equation}
This structure reduces the influence that two closely related indicators would otherwise receive in an equal three-term sum. It does not remove all statistical dependence. Percentiles are conditional on the analysed inventory and epochs; they are not transferable physical thresholds.

For endpoint changes, the lower 20\% tails of NDVI and MNDWI and the upper 20\% tail of NDBI defined three adverse criteria. Their sum identified cells meeting at least two or all three criteria. Because these thresholds are data-derived and the indicators correlated, convergence measures descriptive screening prevalence rather than damaged area or statistical hotspot significance. Threshold and sensor-harmonisation sensitivity records accompany the analysis.

\subsection{Terrain, priority and uncertainty}
Slope, a contributing-area wetness proxy and distance to a thresholded D8 drainage network described terrain context \citep{farr2007srtm,beven1979topmodel}. Higher slope and wetness and shorter drainage distance were oriented towards greater susceptibility. Their component percentiles were averaged to obtain $T$. Flow accumulation represents topographic convergence, not discharge; the DEM cannot resolve all culverts, walls or local drains.

The primary score was $P=0.5L+0.5T$. Equal domain weights were a declared structural choice, not an expert-elicited preference or fitted conservation model. Climate was not used to create fine-scale rank differences. A separate integrated context score was retained as a sensitivity analysis. Scores are dimensionless and relative to the same 17 mapped components.

Scale comparisons retained those components and evaluated rank concordance and top-set overlap across the three radii. Factor and domain ablations recomputed scores after renormalising retained terms. The archived integrated-score weight analysis used 50,000 draws; spatial sensitivity used 2,000 resamples of 150 m spectral blocks with terrain held fixed. Additional notebook experiments assess shared spatial weights, coordinate perturbations with terrain re-extraction, and joint spatial/decision uncertainty. These experiments target different uncertainty sources and are reported separately. Rank intervals and top-three frequencies are conditional simulation summaries, not probabilities of damage. Complete configurations and component results are supplied in the Supplementary Information and machine-readable archive.

\subsection{Climate trends and acquisition windows}
The regional series averages each day equally across the four unique weather cells before annual aggregation; it is not an average over 17 independent component climates. Annual metrics were characterised by Theil--Sen slopes \citep{sen1968slope}. For the corrected annual uncertainty analysis, we fitted a robust linear trend, resampled five-year blocks of its residuals and restored the fitted trend to obtain slope intervals. Null slopes were calculated by adding the same resampled residuals to a constant baseline. The two-sided Monte Carlo probability was $(1+\#\{|\beta_b^0|\geq|\widehat\beta|\})/(B+1)$, with $B=2,000$. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment was applied to the 12 annual metrics \citep{benjamini1995fdr}. This family is distinct from the archived joint annual/seasonal family. The procedure assumes that detrended residual blocks adequately represent short-range dependence; it does not resolve product bias.

Antecedent weather was calculated for every Landsat acquisition over 7, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days and then averaged within each epoch. Product comparisons used matched dates and periods. Weather--spectral correlations involved only five independent composite epochs and were retained as exploratory, without causal interpretation. No weather field was downscaled to the Landsat grid.

\subsection{Retrospective spatial proxy benchmark}
The feature table contained 20,749 class-stratified samples with six reflectance bands and four indices. The established outer split assigned 4,188 samples in block columns 3--4 to testing, excluded 4,467 samples in columns 2 and 5 as a buffer, and retained 12,094 development samples. The 20 test blocks did not overlap the 60 development blocks. The minimum observed sample separation was calculated from grid coordinates. Labels represent WorldCover agreement and carry temporal mismatch with the E2019 predictors.

The extension compared multinomial logistic regression, random forest, extra trees, histogram gradient boosting, a multilayer perceptron, XGBoost and CatBoost \citep{breiman2001rf,pedregosa2011sklearn,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost}. Each family received three predeclared configurations and the same three contiguous inner validation bands. Adjacent block rows were excluded from inner training. Standardisation was fitted inside each training fold; early stopping was disabled to avoid an implicit random validation split. Mean inner macro-F1, computed over all six classes even when a fold omitted a class, selected the primary family and configuration before outer predictions were generated.

The water proxy was geographically concentrated and absent from one inner training fold. We retained this difficult fold, assigned zero predicted mass to an unseen class where necessary, and kept the six-class scoring denominator. This tests transfer under the observed label geography rather than manufacturing class coverage. It also limits the precision of comparisons.

Outer metrics included macro-F1, fixed-six-class mean recall, overall agreement, log loss, Brier score and ten-bin top-label calibration error. Two thousand shared test-block resamples produced paired macro-F1 differences and percentile intervals. A scalar temperature was estimated from development out-of-fold predictions and applied to outer probabilities. Fixed-capacity bands-only, indices-only, location-only, shuffled-label and class-prior controls diagnosed information content; three seeds assessed the selected model's refit sensitivity. These controls did not alter primary selection.

The outer stripe had been inspected in earlier analyses. The extension is therefore retrospective, even though its model selection uses development data only. It does not provide a new untouched test, a population-area accuracy estimate, or independent evidence of monument condition \citep{olofsson2014accuracy,foody2002accuracy}.

\subsection{Execution and provenance}
The new benchmark was executed in Google Colab and independently rerun on Windows using recorded package versions. Colab outputs supply the main comparison table; both executions preserve input hashes, splits, tuning records and predictions. The historical numerical locks remain unchanged, including discrepancies. The audit corrected a notebook climate routine that had shuffled raw annual values instead of detrended residuals; its former intervals and out-of-range probabilities were not retained as inferential results. New regression tests check trend preservation, bounded probabilities, a constant-series null and deterministic seeds. Stored-reference checks are distinguished from checks on newly computed outputs. Raw-to-composite acquisition remains dependent on upstream products; the incomplete standalone integrated bundle is an archived result source, not a self-contained reproduction path \citep{wilson2017practices,nust2018reproducible}.
