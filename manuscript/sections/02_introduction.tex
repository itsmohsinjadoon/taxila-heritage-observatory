\section{Introduction}
Conserving a serial archaeological landscape requires deciding which components to inspect with limited staff and time. Satellite imagery is abundant, but authorities still need a defensible way to turn environmental evidence into a visit order.

Systematic reviews of climate and cultural heritage identify this translation
step, not hazard detection, as the weak link between research and conservation
practice \citep{fatoric2017threatened,orr2021climate}. Deterioration mechanisms
are diverse and site-specific, from Arctic permafrost loss
\citep{hollesen2018arctic} to Mediterranean coastal erosion
\citep{reimann2018mediterranean} and inland fluvial hazard
\citep{liu2019angkor,arrighi2023flood}, and vulnerability depends on materials,
maintenance, drainage and microenvironment as well as forcing
\citep{sesana2021climate,sesana2020vulnerability,crowley2022risk}.

Earth observation and GIS supply the inputs such assessments require, with mature
workflows for landscape survey, hazard screening, disaster-risk management and
monitoring of designated properties in changing environments
\citep{lasaponara2011satellite,opitz2018trends,tapete2018geosciences,agapiou2020eo,luo2023space,guzman2020monitoring,agapiou2021dynamic,hadjimitsis2013cyprus},
increasingly with machine-learning support
\citep{argyrou2022review,yao2023overview,xiao2018geoinformatics}. Applications
reaching a component-level ordering typically combine indicators by weighted
aggregation and report the resulting index or zonation
\citep{yang2023yongtai,guerriero2022derwent,romao2022risk}.

A ranked index rarely shows whether its order reflects landscape differences or analytical choices. GIS multicriteria methods expose aggregation, sensitivity analysis tests weights and spatial representation \citep{malczewski2006gis,feizizadeh2014uncertainty,helton2006sensitivity}, and rank acceptability identifies alternatives preferred under uncertain criteria \citep{lahdelma2001smaa}. These checks matter when spectral proxies correlate, neighbourhoods share observations, inventory points are imprecise and weights are declared rather than measured. Without them, a manager cannot distinguish exposure from a modelling artefact.

Taxila, a serial inscription of 18 Gandharan components in Punjab, Pakistan, faces documented damage and encroachment. Khan et al. report damage at three components from ground geophysics and satellite evidence \citep{khan2022taxila}; Butt et al. map land-cover transition and urban encroachment over 1990--2024 \citep{butt2025taxila}; and Megarry et al. assess surrounding land cover in South Asian World Heritage climate vulnerability \citep{megarry2026landcover}. These studies establish the conservation need but neither an inspection order nor its uncertainty.

CHIP addresses that gap. A hierarchical landscape--terrain rule exposes indicator influence through alternative aggregation rules and factor ablations, with interval estimates on rank comparisons. Multiscale, shared-block, positional and joint experiments test whether priorities survive spatial and decision assumptions. Exact affine reconstruction converts the joint simulation into a balanced full-factorial design for rank acceptability, variance decomposition and an inspection-budget curve. Finally, we compare the ordering with independent published evidence of damage and encroachment at Taxila, regardless of agreement. The aim is to identify which components merit early inspection, how firmly that recommendation is held and which assumption would sharpen it.
