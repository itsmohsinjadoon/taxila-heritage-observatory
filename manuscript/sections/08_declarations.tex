\section*{Permissions, site access and ethics}
The study used only openly licensed satellite, elevation and reanalysis products
together with the published UNESCO component inventory. No fieldwork, sampling,
excavation or physical intervention at the property was undertaken, and no permit
was therefore required. The analytical neighbourhoods are sampling supports
defined for this study; they are not official property polygons and carry no
statutory or UNESCO buffer-zone status. Component coordinates are published
inventory points reported at their published precision.

\section*{Sex and gender in research}
The study analyses satellite, terrain and reanalysis observations of an
archaeological landscape and its inventoried components. It involves no human
participants, no human tissue and no individual-level data, so the sex and gender
dimensions addressed by the SAGER guidelines do not apply to the units of
analysis. The inspection priorities reported here are intended to inform
institutional conservation scheduling and are not disaggregated by any
characteristic of the personnel who would carry out the inspections.

\section*{Data and code availability}
Processed data and analysis code used for the reported results are publicly available in the authors' Taxila Heritage Observatory GitHub repository. The repository URL is supplied to the editor on the separate title page and withheld from this manuscript for double-anonymised review. The repository contains frozen derived rasters, component-level tables, model and uncertainty outputs, code, configurations, source manifests and checksums. Reproduction of the reported analyses begins with the frozen derived evidence. Original third-party Landsat and WorldCover payloads are not redistributed; provider identifiers, acquisition scripts and request manifests document their reacquisition. Code is licensed under MIT and original derived data under CC BY 4.0; third-party materials retain their respective terms. No repository DOI has been assigned.
