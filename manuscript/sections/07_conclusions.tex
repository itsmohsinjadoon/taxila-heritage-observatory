\section{Conclusions}
CHIP links multi-epoch landscape observations to field-inspection priorities while exposing the assumptions behind the ordering. At Taxila, adverse spectral convergence occupied a limited part of common endpoint support, and the preferred inspection order changed with spatial radius, evidence domain and resampling design. Weather-product disagreement and acquisition-window sensitivity constrained climatic explanation. The buffered seven-family comparison supplied a stronger diagnostic of spatial land-cover agreement, but did not validate heritage condition. The practical output is a traceable set of inspection tiers and mechanism-specific questions, to be tested through independent field assessment. Preserving computational corrections and unsuccessful reproduction checks is part of that evidence chain.
