\section{Conclusions}
CHIP links heterogeneous geospatial observations to inspection priorities through an explicit hierarchy and a coordinated experimental assessment. At Taxila, baseline comparisons and ablations showed that moisture-related spectral information and terrain pathways controlled important rank changes. Exact weight crossings quantified the preference intervals sustaining each leading component. Multiscale and structural scenarios identified a persistent core inspection set, while spatial reconstruction and joint simulations exposed additional, mechanism-dependent priorities. Acquisition-matched weather and product comparison constrained climatic interpretation. The separate spatial land-cover experiments demonstrated useful proxy agreement but also competitive simple controls and poor calibration transfer. Together, these results support a component-specific inspection programme with documented uncertainty, providing a practical basis for subsequent field validation and adaptation to other archaeological landscapes.
