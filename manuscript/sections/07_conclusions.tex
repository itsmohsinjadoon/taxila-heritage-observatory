\section{Conclusions}
CHIP converts geospatial observations into inspection priorities through an explicit hierarchy and tests whether the resulting order survives analytical choices.

At Taxila, aggregation mattered: among eight rules using the same six factors, one erased concordance and another reversed the reference order ($\rho=0.038$ and $-0.771$). Moisture-related and terrain indicators dominated, while adverse spectral criteria co-occurred at 1.6--1.8 times their independence expectation.

Exact affine reconstruction yielded a balanced 280,998-scenario design. No component's first-rank confidence factor exceeded 0.42; decision assumptions explained 45.8\% of rank variance versus 31.8\% for spatial support. Coverage of a scenario's three highest priorities rose from 17.9\% with three visits to 51.8\% with four. The variance split identifies which components need a weighting decision and which need better spatial support.

None of five components with published damage or encroachment fell in the structural core tier; Saraikala, with documented core-zone loss, lacked a usable coordinate. CHIP therefore screens moisture- and terrain-driven landscape pressure alongside condition records, with complete inventories essential for prioritisation.

The affine reconstruction, variance decomposition and budget curve transfer to additive weighted-indicator studies, turning an ordering into statements about confidence, unresolved assumptions and inspection capacity.
