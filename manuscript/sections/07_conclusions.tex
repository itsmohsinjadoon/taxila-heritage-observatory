\section{Conclusions}
CHIP links heterogeneous geospatial observations to inspection priorities through
an explicit hierarchy, then subjects that ordering to the tests a conservation
authority would need before acting on it.

At Taxila the ordering proved conditional on the analyst's choices. It is a
property of additively aggregating oriented percentiles: of eight aggregation
rules applied to the same six factors, one gave no relationship and one a
reversal ($\rho=0.038$ and $-0.771$). Within the hierarchy, moisture-related and
terrain pathways controlled the ordering while the vegetation and built/bare
terms were nearly interchangeable, and adverse criteria co-occurred at 1.6--1.8
times the independence expectation.

Recovering the exact affine geometry of the joint simulation converted it into a
balanced 280,998-scenario design at no extra computational cost, yielding three
results a ranking cannot express: no component reached a first-rank confidence
factor above 0.42; decision assumptions accounted for 45.8\% of rank variance
against 31.8\% for spatial support, with the split inverting between components
and so prescribing per component whether a preference decision or a data
acquisition would settle the rank; and a three-component programme covered a
scenario's own three highest priorities in 17.9\% of cases against 51.8\% for
four, pricing the recommendation in visits rather than asserting a tier.

Confronting the ordering with independent evidence changed how it should be used.
None of the five components with published documentation of damage or
encroachment falls in the core tier, and the one with documented core-zone loss
cannot be scored for want of a coordinate. Screening of this kind measures
moisture- and terrain-driven landscape pressure: complementary to damage and
encroachment records rather than a substitute, with inventory completeness a
precondition for prioritisation.

The transferable contribution is procedural: the affine reconstruction, variance
decomposition and budget curve apply to any additive weighted-indicator
prioritisation of heritage assets, need no data beyond what such a study already
produces, and convert a ranking into statements an authority can act on---how
firmly the order is held, which assumption to resolve first, and how many
components must be visited.
